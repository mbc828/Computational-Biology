Figure 1. Evidence workflow. Public records nominate hypotheses, corrected patient transcriptomes provide disease associations, and structural calculations assess model-based compatibility. Five external datasets are contextual references, not equivalent PJI replications. The dashed final box and connector designate pending target-engagement, pathway and perturbation/rescue experiments; no completed mechanistic validation is implied.

Figure 2. Corrected transcriptome. (A) PCA uses the authoritative saved scores for 93 samples (53 PJI, 40 AF); axes show saved explained variance. Black circles identify sample 1364, corrected from AF to PJI, and sample 995, corrected from PJI to AF. (B) Volcano plot of all 13,619 tested genes. Strict differential expression requires BH FDR < 0.05 and absolute log2 fold change > 1: 1,223 upregulated and 261 downregulated genes. Gray points fail one or both criteria. Positive effects indicate higher expression in PJI.

Figure 3. GO BP over-representation using the existing authoritative run, without recalculation. The top 10 of 664 significant UP terms and all seven significant DOWN terms are shown. Ranking is by BH FDR, raw P value and GO identifier. Horizontal position is overlap divided by the direction-specific query size, point area follows 18 + 2 times the overlapping-gene count (pt squared), and color is -log10(BH FDR), with a common scale. Query sizes are 1,036 UP and 242 DOWN Entrez genes; the tested annotated universe comprises 11,997 genes. BH correction covers 5,086 candidate terms per direction. Gene sets use GOALL ancestor propagation from the saved org.Hs.eg.db 3.23.1 and GO.db 3.23.1 analysis. Enrichment does not establish cellular pathway activation.

Figure 4. Prespecified module gene lists from the prespecified downstream result set. Every gene is plotted at its corrected human log2 fold change, with its numerical BH FDR. Orange diamonds satisfy both FDR < 0.05 and absolute log2FC > 1; blue squares meet FDR < 0.05 only; open gray circles have FDR ≥ 0.05. IKBKB is not significant (log2FC = -0.0666, FDR = 0.7095). Dashed reference lines mark log2FC = 1. Module membership is a prespecified descriptive grouping, not a KEGG or module-level statistical test. Shared genes across modules are not independent observations.

Figure 5. External context comparisons from the prespecified downstream result set. (A) Spearman correlations between corrected human PJI effects and all finite matched external effects; n denotes paired genes, not patients. No confidence intervals were available or invented. GSE276597 compares human revision loosening with primary arthroplasty; GSE252481 compares mouse PJI granulocytes with craniotomy controls; GSE295742 compares mouse biofilm-stimulated with unstimulated G-MDSCs; GSE30119 compares human S. aureus infection with healthy whole blood; GSE6269 compares human S. aureus-associated with healthy PBMC samples. (B) Source-scale effect estimates for 16 prespecified genes alongside corrected PJI. Mouse matching uses Heuristic symbols (uppercasing), not validated one-to-one orthology, and is limited to the legacy 8,470-gene intersection. Mouse values are legacy differences in mean log2-transformed counts, not newly fitted edgeR log2FC. Other external effects retain their original methods. Missing S100A8 in GSE276597 is NA. Effects are descriptive and not pooled; no external significance is inferred from this heatmap. Panel B renders np.sign(matrix) with fixed three-level colors: Decrease, No change and Increase; the colorbar label is Direction (source contrast); NA is gray. Effect magnitudes are not directly comparable across columns because the human corrected PJI effects, human external effects and mouse legacy log2-count mean differences arise from different models and scales. Numeric labels retain source-scale values; color encodes direction only and cross-column color intensity must not be interpreted.

Figure 6. MM/GBSA energy estimates shown separately for IKKβ (Baicalein, Wogonin, Berberine and Emodin) and NLRP3 (Baicalein and Berberine). Symbols identify three trajectory-level estimates; black diamonds and whiskers indicate the arithmetic mean and sample SD across three trajectories (ddof = 1), not within-trajectory frame SD or a confidence interval. Fifteen accepted current estimates are supplemented only by three historical Emodin estimates, shown open; nine overlapping historical estimates are excluded, so reanalysis never increases n from 3 to 6. Saved delta_total_kcal_mol values are used consistently. Emodin lacks residue decomposition. The current energy protocol uses igb=5, intdiel=1, extdiel=78.5, saltcon=0.15 and PBRadii=3, without an entropy term; the energy software temperature is 298.15 K versus 300 K in MD. These model-dependent 80–100 ns estimates do not establish affinity, inhibition, cross-receptor selectivity or treatment efficacy.

Figure 7. Follow-up diagnostics from the follow-up structural diagnostic result set. (A) Saved per-trajectory MM/GBSA means in three consecutive 17-frame blocks: 80.0–86.4, 86.8–93.2 and 93.6–100.0 ns. All panels use a common energy scale; 2/15 trajectories have absolute last-minus-first block drift ≥5 kcal/mol. The 5 kcal/mol cutoff is a post hoc descriptive flag, not a prespecified significance or convergence criterion. Blocks are not independent replicates and these diagnostics do not prove convergence. (B) Candidate counts are 5, 7 and 6 for IKKβ Baicalein, Wogonin and Berberine, and 5 and 0 for NLRP3 Baicalein and Berberine. The total of 23 counts system–residue combinations, not 23 unique binding sites. Candidates are the saved post hoc exploratory screen: evaluable three-replicate coverage, negative contributions in 3/3 trajectories and 9/9 blocks, mean contribution ≤-1 kcal/mol, contact occupancy ≥0.5 in each trajectory, and passed identity checks. Contacts use 50–100 ns, unlike the 80–100 ns energy window; missing decomposition is not zero. These thresholds lack independent validation; no new screen is applied. NLRP3–Berberine has only two residues in the three-replicate decomposition intersection, so zero candidates is not evidence of no binding. Emodin lacks decomposition and is excluded.

Figure 8. Coverage and structural descriptors. (A) All 41 unique trajectories in 15 systems are displayed. Thirteen systems have three trajectories; IKKβ–Apigenin and IKKβ–Oroxylin A each have one and are exploratory. Dashes denote absent replicates. (B,C) Each point is a saved nominal 50–100 ns trajectory mean; diamonds and whiskers are the across-trajectory arithmetic mean and sample SD where n = 3. No SD is estimated for n = 1. Protein BB denotes protein backbone self-RMSD. IKKβ/NLRP3 ligand RMSD uses the legacy all-atom definition after legacy protein-backbone fitting. Repaired TLR4–MD2 ligand RMSD uses heavy atoms after protein-backbone fitting and is shown with squares; these definitions are not pooled. Apo ligand RMSD is not applicable, not zero. Only the 12 repaired TLR4–MD2 trajectories are used; all passed main QC, with eight requiring the recorded cutoff-boundary review after automatic supplemental flags. The available Apigenin trajectory ends at 99.8500061 ns; its saved window is preserved, not extended. These descriptors alone establish neither target binding nor trajectory convergence.
